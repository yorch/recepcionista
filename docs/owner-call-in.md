# Owner call-in: update the AI by phone

The business owner calls the business line from their **verified** phone and talks to a private assistant: "we raised the diagnostic fee to $99", "closed Monday", "new FAQ: ...". The assistant reads the change back; after the owner says yes, the server texts a confirmation code to the owner's phone; the change goes live when the owner **replies YES with that code**. Every change is audited and can be undone.

## How the owner uses it

1. Call the business number from the phone verified in Settings. The assistant says it is the private owner line and that no transcript is kept.
2. Say one change at a time. The assistant says it back ("So: Change AC Diagnostic: price $99. Is that right?"). Say yes (or correct it).
3. A text arrives: `Reply YES K7M2QX to apply: ... Reply NO K7M2QX to cancel. UNDO K7M2QX to revert. Expires in 30 min.` The change is **not live** until you reply `YES K7M2QX`. `NO K7M2QX` cancels. After it is applied, `UNDO K7M2QX` reverts it for 30 minutes; later, say "undo my last change" on a call (a fresh code is texted) or press Undo in Her brain > Voice changes.
4. Her brain > **Voice changes** lists every proposal with status (waiting, applied, undone, cancelled) and Apply / Reject / Undo buttons for owners.

Spanish works the same (`SI <code>`, `DESHACER <code>`); the language follows the business default.

What the assistant can change: **services** (add, remove, price, duration, description), **opening hours** (`set_hours`: days plus open and close time, or closed those days), **holiday closures** (`add_closed_dates` / `remove_closed_dates`: exact dates) and the **service area** (`set_service_area`: the full list), all applied to the REAL business fields that booking availability, the scheduler, `createBooking` and reschedule read (a closed date has no slots and refuses bookings), plus informational **notices** (`set_notice`, closure wording only: never for hours or the service area), **FAQs** (add, replace, remove) and **pricing notes** (append a line).

What it cannot do (it says so and points to the dashboard): change the assistant's instructions or system prompt, change the call script, touch phone numbers, web addresses or emails, payments, bookings, customers' data, or anything about other businesses.

## Security model

Caller ID can be spoofed and everything the model says is untrusted, so:

* **Owner mode is entered only on the server.** The initiation webhook / incoming route enter it only when the caller id equals `business.owner_phone_e164` (E.164 equality, anonymous ids refused) **and** `owner_phone_verified_at` is set. It sets `calls.kind = 'owner'` and the per-call owner prompt, first message and `owner_mode` variable. The model can never claim owner status: the three owner tools (`propose_kb_change`, `list_recent_changes`, `undo_last_change`) re-derive it from the database on every call (call row found by the platform's call sid / conversation id, kind `owner`, still running, within `OWNER_CALL_MAX_SECONDS`, stored caller still the verified owner number) and are refused otherwise. An owner call can also not use customer tools (booking, texting, payments).
* **Two channels.** A proposal is only *recorded* from the call. It is applied only when `YES <code>` arrives by SMS at `/api/sms/inbound` (Twilio-signed) **from the verified owner phone**, handled after STOP/START/HELP so opt-outs always win. The code is a 6-character HMAC of (business id, change id) keyed from `APP_SECRET_KEY`: stateless, bound to one business and one change, never spoken on the call, valid 30 minutes, single use (the change status is the state; replays and Twilio retries are no-ops). A spoofed caller id therefore reaches only a conversation: the confirmation goes to the real owner's phone. **Even a call with STIR attestation A skips nothing**: attestation says the carrier vouches for the number, not that the person is the owner (shared phone, SIM swap, forwarded line), and the SMS is the cheap, uniform second factor.
* **Patches are validated** (`src/lib/kb-patch.ts` `validateOwnerKbPatch`, `src/lib/voice/owner/ops.ts`). Only services, FAQs and pricing notes can change, through seven fixed operations; the system prompt and call script can never be touched (an attempt is flagged). New text is scanned and refused when it looks like an instruction (ignore/disregard previous instructions, system prompt, role markers, chat-template tokens, code fences, "you are now", Spanish equivalents, obfuscated spellings), mentions an internal tool name, contains a web address, email or phone number (no allowance: use the dashboard), asks for card numbers/passwords, or has control/zero-width characters. Counts and lengths are capped. The summary the owner hears and is texted is built by the server from the validated values, never taken from the model. The agent prompt is rebuilt from validated knowledge by `buildSystemPrompt`, which wraps these values as untrusted data.
* **Read-back is enforced.** `propose_kb_change confirmed=false` returns the read-back and records that it happened; `confirmed=true` works only for the identical resulting change within 10 minutes, once. The model cannot skip it.
* **Stale proposals** (the knowledge base was saved after the proposal) are refused and cancelled, since a patch holds whole lists and would overwrite newer edits. **Undo** restores the previous values only if the touched fields still hold what the change wrote; otherwise it reports `changed_since` and restores nothing.
* **Limits (per caller-identity class).** 10 owner sessions per day (the 11th gets a short "limit reached" message), 6 confirmation texts per hour and the 1-hour lockout of the owner line after 5 attack-looking proposals are kept **separately for two classes of call**: `verified` (STIR attestation A: the originating carrier vouches for the number, so it cannot be a spoof of the owner's number) and `unverified` (no verdict, B or C: the caller id is spoofable). Both classes are budgeted per business, so a spoofer on an unverified line can use up the unverified sessions, texts and lockout but never the budget of an A-attested owner call (`ownerTrustOf` / `ownerBudgetId` in `src/lib/voice/owner/config.ts`; the class is read from `calls.caller_verified`, stored from the verstat before the first budget check; audit rows say `trust`). A confirmation text is only ever sent to the VERIFIED owner phone and names the time of the call. Unchanged: 5 proposals per call, 5-minute maximum call (`OWNER_CALL_MAX_SECONDS`, clamped to 30-300), 10 SMS command attempts per 15 minutes. All by Postgres limiter / counts. Residual: a real owner whose carrier sends no attestation shares the unverified class with a spoofer and falls back to the dashboard when a spoofer has spent it; no owner PIN was added (a spoken PIN is replayable by whoever the caller is, the SMS reply from the verified phone stays the real control).
* **Audit.** `owner_kb.session`, `proposed`, `applied`, `rejected`, `reverted`, `expired`, `stale`, `refused`, `locked`, `tool_refused`, `undo_requested`, with source `owner_voice` / `owner_sms` / `dashboard` and the change summary (no phone numbers or secrets).

## Privacy

Owner calls keep **no transcript, summary, recording or language**; the provider-side conversation is deleted best effort after the call. They count AI minutes, are never blocked by the minute caps (only an inactive plan closes the line), and are excluded from customer artifacts: Calls list, stats, briefings, gratitude/follow-ups, known-caller and release-recency logic. See docs/compliance.md.

## Provider behaviour and its limits (UNVERIFIED unless stated)

* Per-call prompt / first message / max duration come from the initiation webhook's `conversation_config_override`; the **system prompt override must be enabled on the agent** (`initiationAgentOverrides` requests it when `OWNER_CALLIN_ENABLED` is not `false`; the field names are the same unverified ones as the rest of the initiation setup, see docs/runbook.md section 6).
* ElevenLabs configures **tools per agent, not per call** (the override covers prompt, first message, language, voice, duration; no per-call tool list was found). The owner tools are therefore registered on the agent for everyone and refused server-side unless the call row is `owner`. A customer's model seeing three extra tool names is harmless; a refused call is audited (rate limited) as `owner_kb.tool_refused`.
* The Twilio-webhook path (`/api/voice/incoming`) also marks owner calls and returns the owner override in its JSON, but only the native ElevenLabs line can actually carry it to a live call.
* Whether ElevenLabs keeps audio/transcripts of the call before our deletion request is governed by the agent's retention setting; set it to the minimum.

## Limits and non-goals

One change per proposal. Hours, closures and the service area are written in the SAME transaction as the knowledge-base change, captured in `previous` for undo (refused with the changed fields listed when someone edited them since) and checked against their values at proposal time (`owner_kb_changes.base`; edited since = refused as stale). The same per-field check covers the knowledge-base fields a proposal touches (services, FAQs, pricing notes, call script): confirming one proposal rebuilds the derived system prompt but does NOT make the other pending proposals stale; only a second change to a field that changed since it was proposed is. A voice UNDO of a change of any age (even beyond the 7-day / 100-row window) texts a code that validates. `list_recent_changes` reads out only applied / reverted changes, every confirmation text says when the call was made ("Requested on a call at 3:12 PM. Not you? Reply NO <code>."), and a caller id the carrier reports as `TN-Validation-Failed` never enters owner mode. Overnight hours, several windows per day and closing the whole week are not supported by phone. No multi-owner support beyond the single verified owner phone; a shared phone means anyone with that phone can confirm texts. Turning the feature off: `OWNER_CALLIN_ENABLED=false`.

## Voice reply: "who called?" and "text them back"

The same private owner line can answer customers. The owner gets the usual texts from the assistant (missed call, voicemail, emergency, handoff, booking) and phones the business line from the verified phone. The assistant lists who needs an answer and, on request, texts one of them for the owner. **Nothing reaches a customer until the owner replies `YES <code>` by SMS from the verified phone**, the same second channel as knowledge changes. Off switch: `OWNER_REPLY_ENABLED=false` (default follows `OWNER_CALLIN_ENABLED`).

### How the owner uses it

1. Call the business number from the verified phone. Ask "who called?" The assistant calls `list_recent_leads` and reads up to five numbered leads, e.g. "Number 2: an emergency call from Maria, number ending 4821, today around 3:12 PM." Only a first name, the last 4 digits, the kind (emergency, handed to you, missed, voicemail, call, booking) and the time are read; never a full number, address, summary or anything the customer said.
2. Say "text number 2 that we can come tomorrow at 9". The assistant calls `draft_reply(lead_ref, message, confirmed=false)` and reads back: who it goes to (first name and last 4), the exact words, and that it is sent only after a code and cannot be unsent. After "yes" it calls again with `confirmed=true`.
3. A text arrives: `Reply YES K7M2QX to text Maria (...4821): "We can come tomorrow at 9 AM." NO K7M2QX cancels. Cannot be undone once sent. Expires in 30 min.` The owner sees the exact words. `YES K7M2QX` sends it, `NO K7M2QX` cancels. `UNDO` is answered with "cannot be undone". The owner gets "Sent to Maria." or an honest "Not sent: ..." (opted out, quiet hours, limits, plan allowance).
4. Extras: `mark_lead_handled` ("I dealt with number 2": the assistant reads it back, then you confirm) hides that customer from later lists until they call again; at most 3 per call and 5 per day on an unverified call (30 per day on a carrier-verified one). `get_callback_number` texts the owner the customer's number (to the owner phone only; nothing is sent to the customer and the number is never spoken).
5. The dashboard (Her brain, under Voice changes) lists **Owner voice replies** with the status: waiting for your YES, sending, sent, cancelled, expired, not sent (with the reason), failed, or *unknown: check before resending* (see "Unknown outcomes" below).

Customers receive `<Business name>: <the owner's words>. Reply STOP to opt out`, i.e. the usual identification and STOP footer, as a normal `manual` text (sms_log kind `manual`, consent basis `owner_voice_reply`). The business name is shortened to 40 characters; the owner's words are never cut: a message that would not fit (max 120 characters, at most 4 SMS segments with name and footer) is refused.

### Security model

* **Everything in "Security model" above applies**: server-side owner entry only, `TN-Validation-Failed` refused, no transcript kept, owner session / proposal / lockout budgets. The four tools run only behind the same gate (call row kind `owner`, still running, stored caller = verified owner number) and are refused on every other call. `OWNER_REPLY_REQUIRE_ATTESTATION=true` additionally requires STIR attestation A on the call (default off, since many carriers send no verdict).
* **The model never chooses the recipient.** The tool takes a small lead number (`lead_ref` 1 to 5) that the server issued when it listed leads on THIS owner call (a snapshot in `processed_events`, source `owner-lead-ref`), plus the words. The recipient E.164 is re-derived from the call or booking row of **this business** at draft time and again at send time (a deleted, erased, anonymised or other-tenant row refuses with `lead_gone`). A phone number, name or id in any argument is ignored. The spec of `draft_reply` has no recipient parameter. The owner's own number and the business line are never leads.
* **Two channels, per-reply codes.** A draft only records an `owner_replies` row (`proposed`, 30 minute expiry) and texts the owner a code, the same stateless HMAC as knowledge changes with its own scope (bound to business and reply id), never spoken or returned to the model. `YES` / `NO` are honoured only from the verified owner phone after STOP/START/HELP and the 10-attempts-per-15-minutes limiter. A code applies once: `proposed -> sending` is a single atomic UPDATE, so replays, Twilio retries and racing YES texts send at most once. The daily cap and the identical-text guard are decided in the SAME transaction as that claim, under a per-business advisory lock, and count `sent`, `sending` and `uncertain` rows, so racing YES texts cannot overrun them (8 racing YES texts with a limit of 3 send exactly 3). Codes do not work across businesses, after expiry, after `NO`, or after the send. **A sent text cannot be undone**: the read-back, the confirmation text and the `UNDO` answer all say so; `NO` cancels only before sending.
* **Read-back is enforced** like knowledge changes: `confirmed=true` works only for the identical lead and words read back within 10 minutes, once. An identical reply already waiting is not texted twice.
* **Outbound compliance at send time**, from the database and clock, not from the model: the opt-out list wins (a customer who texts STOP between the draft and the YES is not sent to; refused as `opted_out`); quiet hours 9 PM to 8 AM in the business zone **refuse** (nothing is queued, because a deferred text would outlive the confirmation the owner just gave; the owner is told to ask again after 8 AM); the plan SMS cap blocks and says so; segments are reserved atomically and metered (`sendAndLog`). Consent: the customer called or booked within the last 7 days (the lead window), so this is a caller-initiated 1:1 reply, not marketing.
* **Words are validated** (`reply-text.ts`, on top of the knowledge-change scanner): no links, web or email addresses, `@`, phone numbers, digit runs of 5 or more (verification codes, accounts), spelled-out digit strings, payment or bank requests (gift card, wire, Zelle, Venmo, card number, verification code, Spanish equivalents), emoji or markup, control or zero-width characters, tool names, instruction-like text, chat-template markers. Max 120 characters. This is what stops the line being used to relay phishing or a phone number to a customer. Instruction-like text also counts as an attack toward the existing 5-per-hour lockout; an honest phone number or link is simply refused without a strike. The scanner reads a normalised copy: NFKC (full-width letters and digits), every Unicode decimal digit folded to ASCII (Arabic-Indic, Devanagari, mathematical), spelled digits (English and Spanish) turned into digits so mixed forms such as "917 five five five 0199" count, separators `space . , - / ( )` between digits, vanity numbers (`1-800-FLOWERS`, `555-CALL`), links in words (`dot com`, `slash`, `bitly/abc`, `h t t p`), brand and credential asks (gift card, Zelle, Venmo, Cash App, Apple Pay, Google Pay, SSN, login, password, PIN, card details, deposit link, and the Spanish equivalents) also with spaces, dots or leetspeak squashed out (`Zel le`, `C a s h A p p`, `g1ft c4rd`), and any letter outside the Latin script is refused (Cyrillic or Greek look-alikes of Latin letters). Residual: a determined sender can still find wording that expresses a payment or number in a way no pattern list covers; the owner sees the exact words in the confirmation text before anything is sent, and this is a convenience for honest owners, not a defence against a malicious or compromised owner phone.
* **Limits.** 5 drafts per owner call; drafts per identity class per rolling 24 hours and texts sent per business per rolling 24 hours both at `OWNER_REPLY_DAILY_LIMIT` (default 10); 2 texts per customer per hour; 6 lead lists per call per hour; confirmation texts share the 6 an hour of knowledge changes; the identical text to the same customer within an hour is refused (also while a send is in flight or unknown). Budgets are per identity class (`verified` / `unverified` caller, see config.ts) for drafts and lockouts, so a spoofer cannot spend the budget of an attested owner call.
* **Lead disclosure is capped per identity class (report 16 H1).** A caller who presents the owner's number but no carrier verdict (a spoofed caller id, or an owner whose carrier sends none) is the `unverified` class. The number of DISTINCT leads that class may hear per business per rolling 24 hours is capped (`OWNER_REPLY_DISCLOSED_PER_DAY`: 10 unverified, 30 verified; in `reply-config.ts`, not an env var): a lead already read out to the class is free to list again, a new one counts, and when the budget is spent the older ones are left out and the assistant says so. `mark_lead_handled` needs a read-back and confirmation, is limited to 3 per call and 5 per day (unverified) or 30 per day (verified), and a mark made on an unverified call hides the lead only from unverified listings (a verified owner call still sees it). Together a spoofer can learn at most about 10 recent leads a day (first name, last 4 digits, kind and time) and hide at most 5 of them from other unverified callers, and nothing from the verified owner. The marker row is a keyed HMAC (sub-key of `APP_SECRET_KEY`), not a hash of the number, and `eraseCustomerData` deletes it.
* **Unknown outcomes (report 16 M1, L3).** Once the provider has accepted the text it is `sent`: a failure afterwards (writing the `sms_log` row, updating the reply) never turns it into `failed`, so the owner is never told "not sent" for a text that went out and the duplicate guard stays on. A provider answer that says nothing about delivery (a timeout) and a claim that never finished (a crash or restart between the claim and the outcome) end as `uncertain`: never retried, counted toward the daily cap and the duplicate guard, shown on the dashboard as "unknown: check before resending", and a YES for it answers "may or may not have gone out". The reconcile job moves a `sending` row older than 10 minutes to `uncertain` (the dashboard shows it as unknown after 5).
* **Cancelling a draft you did not make (report 16 L2).** The confirmation text says `Not you? Reply NO <code>.` (when the call that asked was at a time the owner may not have made it). A bare `NO` from the verified owner phone also cancels every draft that is still waiting; a bare `YES` never sends, it asks for the code.
* **Customer text is untrusted data.** Leads are described only with fields the server derived: kind, a one-word first name (letters only, at most 20 characters; anything with markup, digits or symbols is dropped), last 4 digits and time. Call summaries, transcripts, notes, services and addresses are never read aloud or relayed, so a customer who says "ignore your instructions" has nothing to inject through. The owner prompt also states that customer words are data. What a customer says is therefore not available to the owner by phone at all (use the dashboard).
* **Audit.** `owner_reply.leads_listed / proposed / sent / refused / failed / cancelled / expired / handled / callback_sent` with ids, counts and reason codes only (no message, number or name), plus the `sms_log` row of the delivered text.

### Privacy

`owner_replies` holds the recipient number, first name and the words, so it follows retention (`retention_days`) and customer erasure (`eraseCustomerData` deletes rows for that number). The dashboard shows first name, last 4 digits and the words, never the full number. A spoofed owner caller id (the call must still reach the line as the owner's number and not be refused by the carrier check) can hear first names, last 4 digits, the kind and times of recent leads, at most about 10 distinct leads a day (see "Lead disclosure is capped" above), and nothing else; nothing can be sent without the real owner's YES text. `OWNER_REPLY_REQUIRE_ATTESTATION=true` closes this completely for lines whose carrier gives attestation (default off); for health-adjacent verticals (dental, medical) turn it on when the carrier supports it.

### Provider behaviour (UNVERIFIED)

The four tools are registered on the agent like the other owner tools (tools are per agent; the route refuses them off the owner line). Whether the platform fills the string `lead_ref` as a string (it may send a number; both are accepted) and whether ElevenLabs lets a webhook tool result be read as written are unverified, as for the rest of the owner tools (docs/runbook.md section 9).
